Nvidia's CEO exits and the stock slides
Nvidia ended the week at −12.8%, a slide that began with a board investigation on Nov. 8 and continued after Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on Nov. 12. It closed Nov 13 at $162.85, which is −31.9% from its Oct 6 high.
The slide began on Sunday, Nov. 8. Nvidia's board said it was opening an independent investigation after reports that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. Huang also emailed employees that day and admitted to an affair. On Thursday, Nov. 12, Nvidia said Huang had resigned as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor. Shares fell as much as 14% that day. They closed at $166.19, down 4.34%, on volume of 734.3 million shares. Friday added another −2.0%, and the chip index (SOX) fell −3.4% as the selloff spread. For the week, Nvidia ended −12.8%. Over about a month it is −25.0%. Its market value is about $3.92T, still #2 among the tracked stocks behind Apple at $5.18T. Reuters reported the figure was about $5.8 trillion in early October.

2. Why it moved
CEO exit and probe
14%
Nvidia's week started on Sunday, Nov. 8, before the resignation. That day the board said it was opening an independent investigation after reports that founder and CEO Jensen Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort, per Reuters. Huang also emailed employees and admitted to an affair. 'There is no excuse for what I did,' he wrote. Reuters noted the New York Times had reported in October that he had a relationship with Alice Huang, an unrelated former executive of Singapore-based Megaspeed. On Thursday, Nov. 12, Nvidia said Huang had resigned as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately, ending 33 years as CEO. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO and keeps her CFO job while the board searches for a permanent successor. CFO Dive reported shares fell as much as 14% on the resignation news. They closed that day at $166.19, down 4.34%, on volume of 734.3 million shares, well above the three-month average, per Yahoo Finance. Reuters reported Nvidia's market value fell from about $5.8 trillion in early October to roughly $4 trillion as of Thursday. Reports list the events and the price moves side by side. None divides the week's drop between the investigation and the resignation.
CFO Dive · Nov 12
Analyst downgrades
$200
Several brokers changed their views on Nov. 12 and 13. Bernstein downgraded Nvidia to Market-Perform and halved its price target to $200. Oppenheimer downgraded the stock and removed its price target. HSBC cut it to hold from buy with a $170 target, citing uncertainty over the leadership succession. Not every firm turned negative on the rating. Stifel kept Buy and cut its target to $205 from $315. Citi kept Buy and lowered its target to $225. Opinions split, sometimes inside one firm. Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon said he was not worried about near-term impacts to the business and that the shares 'screen extremely cheap'. He also warned of 'considerable uncertainty' around further news flow. Baird's Tristan Gerra listed possible further senior management departures and H200 write-down charges as headwinds, and said the episode hasn't hit its bottom yet. Those calls came in the same days the stock fell, and the reports do not say how much of the drop they explain. Nvidia closed Nov. 13 at $162.85.
Investing.com · Nov 12
Chip selloff spreads
5.1%
The selloff did not stay inside one stock. On Thursday, Broadcom and AMD rose more than 5% each as Nvidia fell, per Schwab. On Friday that flipped. In afternoon trading the Philadelphia SE Semiconductor index was down 5.1%, a more than $560 billion wipeout, with AMD down 10.7% and Broadcom down 5.9%, Reuters reported. By the close, the chip index (SOX) was −3.4% on the day and Broadcom was −4.8%. Both still ended the week higher, at +8.1% and +18.2%. The Nasdaq-100 was down 2% in the afternoon, on pace to erase about $800 billion in market value, Reuters said. It closed the day at −1.2% and ended the week at +3.5%. Reuters also reported Nvidia was on track for its longest streak of weekly losses since October 2012. Its headline for the day read 'Neoclouds, AI stocks slide again as Nvidia turmoil rattles sector.' That is Reuters's framing of the link. The closing figures were smaller than the afternoon snapshots, and the index still finished the week higher.

A lopsided week
Most of the group finished the week higher. Broadcom led at +18.2%, followed by Tesla at +8.9%, Meta at +5.2%, Apple at +4.1%, Alphabet at +3.9% and Amazon at +2.8%. Microsoft slipped −1.3%. Nvidia ended −12.8%, the weakest of the group. The Nasdaq-100 gained +3.5% and the S&P 500 gained +2.5%. The chip index (SOX) rose +8.1% on the week. 3. The next 7 days
Tuesday, Nov. 17, is the big date. Nvidia reports fiscal third-quarter results, with a call at 5 p.m. ET. Analysts expect earnings growth of 90% on revenue of $109 billion, per Reuters. It is the first quarterly report since Huang resigned. Bernstein's Rasgon says he is not worried about near-term business impact. Baird's Gerra lists H200 write-down charges and possible further senior departures as headwinds. The report gives the first quarterly figures to set against both views. October retail sales also arrive Tuesday. On Wednesday, Nov. 18, the Fed releases minutes of its Oct. 27-28 meeting. Traders are fully pricing in a December rate hike, with odds of a 50-basis-point move around 7%, per CME FedWatch. Stock Titan noted October core CPI of 0.3% met the level Fed Governor Christopher Waller had set, and quoted him saying 'a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered at the December meeting.' Anthropic is set to price its IPO on Nov. 18 and begin trading on Nasdaq as ANT on Thursday, Nov. 19. It is selling 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each, up to $64.4 billion. Reuters calls Nvidia's results and the IPO tests for the AI trade.
