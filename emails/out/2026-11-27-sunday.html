Sunday report · Nov 23–27, 2026
Huang's return and a rebound lifted Nvidia
Nvidia rose 19.9% this week, helped by its board reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO and by a market-wide rebound. The shares jumped 31.48% on Monday alone, more than the week's total.
Nvidia closed Friday at $150.49, up 19.9% on the week. Monday, Nov 23, was the big day. The board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president, CEO and director, and the shares surged 31.48%, adding about $950 billion in market value. That one-day gain was larger than the week's total, so the rest of the week netted lower. Huang had resigned 11 days earlier following allegations of affairs. The board said it acted after input from senior levels of the US government. Analysts upgraded the stock the same day, and the wider market rallied. The Nasdaq closed up 6.51% on Monday and the S&P 500 rose 4.73%. Wednesday brought a sharp drop in tech after October inflation data, with QQQ down 4.17% by 9:40 a.m. ET. On Friday, Nvidia added 0.8% and the chip index (SOX) was up 9.2% on the week. The rebound still leaves Nvidia 37.1% below its Oct 6 high, and down 30.1% over about a month. Its market value is about $3.63T, third among the stocks tracked here.

2. Why it moved
Huang's reinstatement
+31.48%
The main company-specific driver was one board decision. On Monday, Nov 23, Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president, CEO and director. Huang had resigned just 11 days earlier, following allegations of affairs, according to the Motley Fool. The shares surged 31.48% that day, adding about $950 billion in market value, TheStreet reported. The board said it voted after discussions with Huang and input from senior levels of the US government. It added that it is confident his reinstatement poses no national security risk. The statement also said the committee's investigation will proceed to conclusion, and that Nvidia will keep cooperating with government investigations and inquiries. So the CEO question closed while others stayed open. Citi's Atif Malik framed it this way: the reinstatement removes the key overhang created by the CEO transition. The weekly numbers need care. Monday's jump was larger than the full-week gain of 19.9%, so the other days combined netted lower, even though Friday added about 0.8%.
SEC.gov · Nov 23
Analysts reverse course
48 of 65
Analysts changed their calls after Huang's return. StoneX's Cody Acree upgraded Nvidia to Buy from Hold on Nov 23 with a $270 target. That is $65 below his prior $335 estimate, which StoneX halted after Huang resigned. TheStreet's headline called that a surprising catch. Oppenheimer's Rick Schafer upgraded to Outperform with a $250 target. Melius Research's Ben Reitzes upgraded to Buy and raised his target to $220 from $185. Citi's Atif Malik raised his target to $240 from $225. Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon upgraded the stock back to Outperform, though he also called the board's on-again-off-again response clown-like. Yorkville's Dan Ives said the stock should be valued at $300 with Huang back. According to LSEG data cited by CNBC, 48 of 65 analysts covering Nvidia rated its shares Buy or Strong Buy. The sources do not settle whether the notes pushed the price or followed it.
TheStreet · Nov 25
Market-wide rebound
+5.6%
Nvidia did not rise alone. The week before, the S&P 500 fell 8.61%, its largest weekly drop since April 4, 2025. Monday's rebound was broad. The Nasdaq closed up 6.51%, the S&P 500 rose 4.73% and the Dow gained 3.01%, according to Wallstreetcn via Longbridge, which tied the move to Huang's return. Wednesday gave some back. October PCE inflation was 3.8%, matching forecasts, but core PCE of 3.3% topped the 3.2% forecast. By 9:40 a.m. ET, QQQ was down 4.17%. Polymarket priced a quarter-point Fed hike for December at 93%. Friday's shortened session helped. The Nasdaq climbed 2.31% to 24,129.50 as chipmakers led, and Marvell jumped 7.34% to $140.54. For the week, the S&P 500 rose 5.6% and the Nasdaq rose 6.68%. Nvidia's 19.9% gain was well above both, so the stock moved with the market and then went further.
Longbridge · Nov 27
Every stock rose
All eight tracked stocks finished the week higher. Nvidia led at +19.9%, followed by Tesla at +15.7% and Meta at +12.2%. Those three beat the Nasdaq-100's +6.8%. Apple rose +4.7% and closed at its yearly high. Alphabet gained +3.6%, Amazon +3.0%, Broadcom +2.6% and Microsoft +1.7%. Amazon closed up +4.0% on Friday. By mid-morning its shares were up 2.96%, as Adobe reported $7.1 billion in Thanksgiving Day online spending. The month still looks rough for several names. Nvidia is −30.1% over about a month and Microsoft is −7.2%.

The board said its own committee investigation will proceed to conclusion. Citi's Atif Malik, on the other side, argues the reinstatement removed the key overhang from the CEO transition. The same day brings ISM manufacturing PMI and JOLTS job openings data. For context, Polymarket priced a quarter-point Fed hike after the December meeting at 93% following the PCE report, and the 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.94% on Friday. None of these events has a result yet.
